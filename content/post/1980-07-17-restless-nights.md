Recorded in the early days of 1980, “Restless Nights” treads similar lyrical ground as “The River,” which is probably why it didn’t make the album.

Both songs look back on a love that started fierce and true but grew cold and remote with time. But unlike the classic title track, “Restless Nights” cloaks its mourning in jangly power-pop trappings.

{{< youtube DCFYLa02WzE >}}

I know we usually focus more of our attention on Bruce’s lyrics than his arrangements, but here’s a case where the muscular music outshines a story Bruce tells more powerfully elsewhere. And that’s okay, because musically, “Restless Nights” is a master class in E Street arrangement and tension.

The track kicks off with an urgent, guitar-and-organ pairing that feels cut from the same cloth as “Loose Ends,” reminiscent of early Tom Petty or the British Invasion. Roy Bittan's bright piano and Danny Federici's swirling organ give the track an uplifting, almost buoyant momentum.

Yet underneath that pop propulsion lies a weeping undertow. Bruce sings with deep vulnerability, while Steven Van Zandt adds plaintive harmony on the chorus. The contrast of a driving, hook-laden melody wrapped around lyrics of haunted insomnia creates a melancholy pop confection that Little Steven still considers one of his friend’s finest songs.

Lyrically, nothing really happens in “Restless Nights.” It’s not so much a story as it is an impressionistic painting of two people who wake on the other side of youth to find their magic gone.

> *Once we ran and lived as one*<br>
> *In another world, my little one*<br>
> *Where whispering trees and dark rivers run*<br>

> *My baby, she has restless nights*

> *On late-night movie screens*<br>

> *Lost in wide-wake dreams*<br>

> *Once I held you in my arms*<br>

> *Once we ran and lived as one*<br>
> *In different world, my little one*<br>
> *Where whispering trees and dark rivers run*<br>

Our narrator is haunted by his memories of young love, taunted even by his television. On screen, lovers stay young forever. In life, we grow old. And in “Restless Nights” growth is just a long process of dying.

The song ends with our narrator wishing for nothing more than one last chance for one last dance of passion, but he knows that’s just a dream.

And the tragic irony of “Restless Nights” is that our lovers can’t even dream, because they can’t sleep. This is a song that captures the 3:00 AM sadness that sets in when you look across the bed at the person next to you and realize that you’re living in love’s ashes.

“Restless Nights” was ultimately left off *The River,* but its warmth and grace makes it one of the strongest outtakes from that era.

It’s also one of the rarest, performed live only once ever, on the last night of the Working on a Dream Tour, which also happened to be Little Steven’s birthday.

For his birthday, Bruce let his best friend pick a song, and Stevie chose one he’d urged Bruce to play for years–his favorite song in Bruce’s catalog, a song he’d lobbied long, hard, and ultimately without success for the album.

In its only live performance (which I was fortunate to witness), “Restless Nights” soared and swooped, forcing Bruce to sing at the top of his register, a challenge he met with grace and to a rapturous reception.

“Dammit, he might have been right all these years,” Bruce admitted afterward.

He probably was.
